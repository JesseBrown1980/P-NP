## Wide boundary scale gate (6–10 bits)

The second native quotient transfer passed all hosted jobs at [run 46151622306](https://github.com/JesseBrown1980/P-NP/actions/runs/46151622306), commit ca6561d. Twenty cells cover widths w=6+floor(cell/4), density 1,3,6,12 by cell mod 4, with 2w chronological windows of w+1 bits. Three distinct variable positions are chosen from lexicographically ordered triples; signs and triples use successive xorshift64 outputs (13,7,17), seed 2026112801+100003*cell. No equivalences are planted. At exactly w steps, the initial and final w-bit strings uniquely determine the full assignment, so independently evaluate original literals on their concatenation. For 2w steps an incoming-edge dynamic recurrence visits all endpoint states and both predecessor states at each step. Local independent NumPy reference used direct literal evaluation for A/B, checked both with the incoming recurrence, and froze the full C=A⊗B recurrence and adversary in [BOUNDARY_SCALE_GITRAM_EXPECTED.hbp](fixtures/BOUNDARY_SCALE_GITRAM_EXPECTED.hbp). Native code checks A and B against incoming recurrence, C from normalized quotient composition against full recurrence, and H against explicit literal violations and Hamming distance. All 20 binary outputs must match the frozen hashes in fan-in, including size, source SHA, unique cell IDs, missing/duplicate/tamper controls. Local oracle is research evidence, not Rust acceptance.

**Exact worst-case obstruction for this quotient.** For w crossing variable pairs, let F(x,y) be the conjunction over i of (!x_i OR y_i) AND (x_i OR !y_i). Its violation-count kernel H(x,y) is Hamming distance. Every row has minimum zero at the unique column y=x; therefore no two distinct rows have identical normalized profiles, and the quotient requires 2^w classes. A CNF with only 2w clauses can defeat this explicit row-equivalence compression. This is a lower bound for this representation, not a lower bound for arbitrary SAT algorithms: equality constraints admit efficient symbolic treatment. No claim of P=NP or mass gap follows.
